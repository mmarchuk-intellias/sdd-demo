# US-402 · Pilot outcomes the data room can trust

As the Amperlane CEO preparing the Series A data room, I want migrated pilot bookings re-classified so that rebooked,
no-show and cancelled are distinct outcomes, with each re-classified record keeping its original status and the rule
and evidence used, and records from before 2026-02-01 flagged for data quality, so that the figures investors see are
not inflated by bookings rebooked after a charger fault or skewed by the early status changes, and every changed
figure can be traced back to its evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

Scenario: S1 (happy path) A booking rebooked after a charger fault no longer counts as a no-show
  Given a pilot booking at Gowanus that Airtable shows as a no-show
  And the site's pilot Google Sheet shows the same driver placed in another slot after a charger fault that day
  When the migration re-classifies statuses
  Then the booking's outcome is rebooked, not no-show
  And it keeps its original status "no-show" together with the rule applied and the Sheet evidence used

Scenario: S2 (happy path) An early no-show recorded as "cancelled"
  Given a pilot booking with the status "cancelled" and a comment saying the driver did not arrive
  When the migration re-classifies statuses
  Then the booking's outcome is no-show
  And it keeps its original status "cancelled" together with the rule applied and the comment used as evidence

Scenario: S3 (failure) No evidence supports a re-classification
  Given a pilot booking with the status "cancelled" and no comment or other record of what happened
  When the migration re-classifies statuses
  Then the booking is not re-classified on a guess
  And it keeps its original status with no re-classification rule or evidence attached

Scenario: S4 (failure) Someone tries to overwrite the original status
  Given a re-classified pilot booking
  When anyone tries to change or remove its original status
  Then the change is refused
  And the original status, rule and evidence are unchanged

Scenario: S5 (edge case) The data-quality boundary
  Given one migrated booking whose slot started on 2026-01-31 and one whose slot started on 2026-02-01, Eastern time
  When the migration runs
  Then the first booking carries the data-quality flag
  And the second does not

Scenario: S6 (edge case) An early booking that is also re-classified
  Given a pilot booking whose slot started before 2026-02-01 and that is re-classified from no-show to rebooked
  When it is migrated
  Then it carries both the data-quality flag and its re-classification with original status, rule and evidence

Scenario: S7 (edge case) A real cancellation stays a cancellation
  Given a pilot booking with the status "cancelled" and a comment saying the driver cancelled by text before the slot
  When the migration re-classifies statuses
  Then the booking's outcome is cancelled, not no-show
  And it keeps its original status "cancelled", with no re-classification rule or evidence attached

## Notes

- "Rebooked" is the glossary term for the pilot's "moved" bookings (REQ-228). Evidence: Airtable still shows bookings
  rebooked after the Gowanus fault as no-shows (SRC-C2@00:52:53); early no-shows were "cancelled" with a comment
  (SRC-C2@00:05:22).
- Depends on CF-018 (pilot figures are reported, uncleaned), Q-029 (is "cancelled" a separate outcome; how S3 records
  are reported), A-026 and Q-020.
- Showing metrics with or without pilot data is REQ-225; the data-room report is REQ-095 (both EP-11).
- Red team: a rule "every commented 'cancelled' becomes no-show" passes S2 and S3 but merges two outcomes REQ-228
  keeps apart; S7 closes it. The 2026-02-01 boundary is applied to the slot start in the site's time zone (a design
  detail for Step 3.3), as US-327 does for month boundaries.
